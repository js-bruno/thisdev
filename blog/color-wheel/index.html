color theory, briefly

harmonies

Harmonies are colors that sit at fixed angles on the wheel. Complementary pairs sit 180° apart and give maximum contrast. Split-complementary takes the two neighbors of that opposite point — softer, easier to balance. Analogous colors are adjacent (here ±25°) and feel calm. Triadic spreads three colors 120° apart, tetradic uses 60°/180°/240°, and square uses 90° steps. When one color dominates, the others read as accents.

shades, tints and tones

Shades add black, tints add white, tones add gray. All three keep the hue and lower the drama, which is why they are useful for backgrounds, borders and hover states. Pick one saturated accent, then fill the rest of the interface with tints, shades and tones of it.

primary, secondary and tertiary

In the traditional painter's wheel, primaries are red, yellow and blue; secondaries are the mixes of two primaries (orange, green, purple); and tertiaries mix a primary with a neighboring secondary (vermilion, amber, chartreuse, teal, violet, magenta). Screens mix light instead of pigment, so the wheel above is RGB-based — same relationships, different primaries.

using this wheel

Drag on the disc to set hue and saturation, use the outer ring for lightness, then rotate the whole harmony by dragging any handle. Paste a hex into the field to start from an existing color, copy any code from the table, and export the palette when it feels right.
